Multiple sequence alignment (MSA) scales poorly with dataset size and introduces systematic errors under insertions and deletions (indels), yet alignment-free methods have historically underperformed MSA-based maximum-likelihood approaches. We present Fusang: Tardigrade Edition, an alignment-free phylogenetics tool that infers trees directly from unaligned sequences via k-mer frequency vector cosine distances (spaced and contiguous patterns, systematically evaluated here), with automatic parameter selection, a multi-k distance ensemble that removes manual k choice, and a random-forest classifier that maps its domain of applicability (150/150 simulated scenarios). On simulated indel-rich data at n=200, Fusang is numerically, but not statistically, more accurate than FastTree2 (nRF=0.199±0.030 vs 0.205±0.030, 130 seeds, Wilcoxon p=0.0507); the comparison reverses at n=1000 under the DCM+EPA pipeline, with FastTree2 significantly better, and MSA methods retain a clear advantage on clean data at all scales. The multi-k ensemble reduces topological error by 8.7% over single-k (30 seeds, p=0.00206), reaching within ~11% of MAFFT+FastTree2 without an alignment, though not matching it. On a real indel-rich mitochondrial benchmark (25 genomes, 9.1% gaps), the ensemble reaches the community-benchmark ceiling (nRF=0.045). MinHash is far less accurate than k-mer cosine at gene length (indel nRF=0.403 vs 0.193) and degrades under indels where k-mer cosine improves. On 11 protein families, k-mer frequency methods outperform context-matching by 2.2–2.4×. Fusang infers trees for 10,000 taxa in ~70 s on a 4-core workstation, with pre-compiled binaries for Windows and Linux.
