Multiple sequence alignment (MSA) scales poorly with dataset size and introduces systematic errors under insertions and deletions (indels), yet alignment-free methods have historically underperformed MSA-based maximum likelihood (ML) approaches. We present Fusang: Tardigrade Edition, an alignment-free phylogenetics tool that infers trees directly from unaligned input — with automatic parameter selection, a multi-k distance ensemble that removes manual k choice, and a random-forest boundary classifier that maps its domain of applicability. Its inference engine, k-mer frequency vector cosine distances (spaced and contiguous patterns, systematically evaluated here), is numerically, but not statistically, better than FastTree2 on simulated indel-rich data at n=200 (indel rate=0.02: nRF=0.199±0.030 vs 0.205±0.030, 130 seeds, Wilcoxon p=0.0507 — borderline at α=0.05, not significant after correction, and reversed at n=1000 indel, where FastTree2 is significantly better), while MSA methods retain a significant advantage on clean data at every scale tested. The multi-k ensemble reduces topological error by 8.7% relative to single-k under indel-rich simulation (nRF=0.137±0.023 vs 0.150±0.026, 30 seeds, p=0.00206), reaching to within ~11% of MAFFT+FastTree2 (0.124±0.026) without alignment but not matching it (p=0.00247 in favour of MSA+ML). On a real indel-rich mitochondrial benchmark (25 genomes, 9.1% gaps), the ensemble reaches the community-benchmark ceiling — nRF=0.045, matched by MSA+ML and all best published alignment-free methods on this single small dataset. MinHash (Mash) is far less accurate than k-mer cosine on gene-length data in both conditions (clean 0.349 vs 0.236; indel 0.403 vs 0.193; 30 seeds, Fusang better in 30/30 seeds, d≈3.1 and 2.6), and the two respond to indels in opposite directions — Mash degrades modestly (1.16×) while k-mer cosine improves (0.83×). At gene length, contiguous k=7 (0.194) and the multi-k ensemble (0.194) are both significantly more accurate than Fusang's default spaced+NNI pipeline (0.199, p=0.0039 and 0.0077), which we therefore present as a robust rather than an optimal default. Cross-domain validation on 11 SwissTree protein families confirms k-mer frequency methods outperform context-matching by 2.2–2.4× (p=0.0010–0.0020). The boundary classifier separates homogeneous from structured datasets (150/150 simulated scenarios). Fusang infers trees for 10,000 taxa in ~70 s on a 4-core workstation, with pre-compiled binaries for Windows and Linux.
